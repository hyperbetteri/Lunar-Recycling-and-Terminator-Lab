# Earlier Ideas: Space Debris Capture with Contactless Force Fields

**Idea sketch (November 2024) — corrected, technically reviewed version**

Author: Almási Andor · Original sketch: November 2024 · Corrected version: October 2026

> This document presents the precursor of the lunar lab: in late 2024 the idea started with the contactless capture of space debris orbiting Earth. In the original text, drafted with the help of AI engines, the physical explanation was wrong at one point; this has been corrected here.

## 1. The problem

Tens of thousands of tracked pieces of space debris orbit Earth, together with far more small fragments. They endanger operational satellites, and collisions create further debris. One difficulty of capture is that old satellites and rocket stages often **spin and tumble**, which makes them hard to grab.

## 2. The original idea

The combined use of two contactless force fields, powered by solar energy:

1. **Rotating magnetic field** for metallic pieces.
2. **Electrostatic field** for non-magnetic pieces (plastic, composite).

## 3. What is the correct physical explanation?

### 3.1. Rotating magnetic field: it does not attract, it slows down

The original text stated that the rotating magnetic field "attracts" metals. For most space debris this is **not true**: aluminium, of which most satellite structures are made, is not ferromagnetic, so a magnet does not pull it.

The real effect is different, and that is exactly what makes it useful: a changing magnetic field induces **eddy currents** in the metal, and according to the fundamental law of electromagnetic induction (Lenz's law) these **brake the object's motion relative to the field**. Eddy-current brakes on trains and trucks work on the same principle. This makes it possible to slow down the rotation of a spinning, tumbling satellite wreck without contact. The literature calls this **eddy-current detumbling**, and it is a genuinely researched technology. Once stabilised, the wreck can then be grabbed safely.

### 3.2. Electrostatic field: contactless towing

The electrostatic force between the charged capture unit and the likewise charged object provides a **weak but continuous** pulling force. From a distance of a few tens of metres it can slowly move even massive objects, for example from geostationary orbit to a slightly higher, so-called graveyard orbit, where retired satellites no longer interfere with operational ones. In the literature this is the concept of the **electrostatic tractor**.

### 3.3. Solar power

Solar panels are indeed suitable for powering the system. Strong magnetic fields, however, require high current and cooling, so the power requirement must always be verified by calculation.

## 4. The corrected concept

| Step | Means | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Approach | Capture unit with solar power supply | Get close to the wreck |
| 2. Detumbling | Rotating magnetic field (eddy-current braking) | Slow down the spin without contact |
| 3. Relocation | Electrostatic towing, or capture with an arm or net | Change the wreck's orbit |
| 4. End goal | Deorbiting with controlled atmospheric re-entry, graveyard orbit, or processing | Eliminate the hazard |

## 5. Why did this lead to the lunar lab?

As the idea was developed further, the question arose of **utilising** the collected material instead of burning it up in the atmosphere. This led to the concept of the [terminator-following lunar lab](01_Terminatorkoveto_holdi_labor.en.md). Calculations show, however, that transporting debris from Earth orbit to the Moon is very energy-intensive, so lunar processing relies primarily on waste generated at the lunar base (see: [02 – LunaRecycle concept](02_LunaRecycle_hulladekfeldolgozasi_koncepcio.en.md)).
